Ant colony optimisation

Run it step by step

Real ants find short paths by leaving pheromone: short routes are travelled more often, collect more pheromone, and attract more ants. Ant colony optimisation copies this with artificial ants building tours on a pheromone map.

⎙Tip: press Ctrl+P to print or save this page as PDF.

01Definition

Each ant builds a tour, choosing the next city j from i with probability proportional to τᵢⱼ^α · (1/dᵢⱼ)^β. After all ants finish, pheromone evaporates by a factor (1−ρ) and each ant adds Q/L on the edges of its tour.

02Pseudocode

τ ← τ0 on every edge;  bestTour ← nil
repeat for t iterations
    place m ants on cities
    each ant: repeat n−1 times
        choose next j with P(i→j) = τij^α ηij^β / Σ τih^α ηih^β,  η = 1/d
    update bestTour
    τ ← (1 − ρ) τ                         # evaporation
    for each ant, for each edge of its tour: τ += Q / L(ant)
return bestTour

03Line by line

τ ← τ₀ on every edgeNo preference at the start.

τ ← (1 − ρ) τEvaporation forgets old information.
τᵢⱼ += Q / Lₖ for edges of ant kShorter tours (small L) deposit more.

04Formulas and why they hold

P(i→j) = τᵢⱼ^α ηᵢⱼ^β / Σ over unvisited h of τᵢₕ^α ηᵢₕ^β, η = 1/d

pheromone strength times visibility, normalised

α weighs learned experience, β weighs the greedy distance heuristic.

τᵢⱼ ← (1 − ρ) τᵢⱼ + Σₖ Q / Lₖ

evaporate, then deposit in proportion to tour quality

Edges shared by many short tours grow; unused edges fade.

05Why it works, and when it does not

Combines a greedy heuristic with experience shared through the environment. Large α can lock in early choices; large ρ forgets quickly and keeps exploring.

06Properties

completeno
optimalno
timeO(m · n²) per iteration
spaceO(n²)

07Traps

  • Normalise only over unvisited cities.
  • Evaporate before depositing (course order).
  • β = 0 ignores distance; α = 0 ignores pheromone and gives a randomised nearest neighbour.
