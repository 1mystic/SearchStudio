Week 7 · Game playing

k-ply tic-tac-toe (static evaluator, horizon effect)

Run it step by step

Real games are too deep to search to the end, so a program searches only k plies ahead and judges the positions at that frontier with a static evaluation function. In tic-tac-toe a simple evaluator counts the lines that are still open: e(n) = (rows, columns and diagonals not yet blocked for MAX) − (those not yet blocked for MIN). Minimax then backs these numbers up. The weakness is the horizon effect: a loss (or win) that arrives just beyond k plies is invisible, so a shallow search can happily walk into it.

⎙Tip: press Ctrl+P to print or save this page as PDF.

01Definition

MAX is X, MIN is O. A line is open for a player if it contains none of the opponent's marks. e(n) is the number of lines open for MAX minus the number open for MIN; a won position is +∞ (MAX) or −∞ (MIN) and a full board without a winner is 0. A k-ply search looks k moves ahead (counting the move being chosen), evaluates the frontier positions with e and backs up with max at MAX nodes and min at MIN nodes.

02Pseudocode

e(n) = (rows, columns, diagonals open for MAX) − (those open for MIN)
        +∞ if MAX has three in a row, −∞ if MIN has
Search(n, depth):
    if n is won or full: return its value (±∞ or 0)
    if depth = 0:        return e(n)            ← the horizon: static evaluation
    MAX node: max over moves of Search(child, depth − 1)
    MIN node: min over moves of Search(child, depth − 1)
choose the root move with the best backed-up value (ties: alphabetical cell order)

03Line by line

for each root move, search k − 1 plies below itEach candidate move is scored by what the best play within the horizon leads to.
at the frontier (depth 0) return e(n)Count the lines open for X and for O and subtract.
a won position is ±∞, a full board is 0Real outcomes are not estimated: they are exact.
back up: max at MAX nodes, min at MIN nodesEach player is assumed to pick the reply that is best for themselves, as far as the horizon sees.
pick the best root move; ties go to the alphabetically first cellA1 before A2 before B1 and so on.
compare with full-depth searchIf the k-ply choice is worse under full search, the evaluator was fooled by the horizon.

04Formulas and why they hold

e(n) = openlines(MAX) − openlines(MIN)

lines still winnable for X minus lines still winnable for O

More winnable lines means more chances to win and fewer to lose. The empty board has 8 lines open for each side, so e = 0; X alone in the centre keeps 8 lines but takes 4 from O, so e = 8 − 4 = 4.

v(n, d) = e(n) if d = 0; max or min over children of v(child, d − 1) otherwise

depth-limited minimax

Minimax is exact only at the leaves of the game; at the frontier it trusts e.

05Why it works, and when it does not

It is how chess programs and every game player with a big tree work: look as deep as the time allows and guess at the frontier. The horizon effect is the price: the guess is only as good as e, and some dangers (forks, forced sequences) take more plies to show up than the horizon allows.

06Properties

completeno (only looks k plies ahead)
optimalonly when k reaches the end of the game
timeO(b^k)
spaceO(k)

07Traps

  • k counts plies (single moves), not pairs of moves: k = 2 is your move and one reply.
  • Values are always from MAX's point of view. When O is to move, O picks the smallest value, not the largest.
  • A higher k is not a guarantee: the evaluator can still be wrong at the frontier. Only the full-depth search is exact.
  • e counts open lines, not threats: two marks in a row and one mark in a row look similar to it, which is exactly why it misses forks.
