Random walk

Run it step by step

Hill climbing is trapped by local maxima; the opposite extreme is a walk that ignores h entirely. A random walk picks a neighbour uniformly at random and always moves there. It explores everything eventually, but only by luck does it hit a solution.

⎙Tip: press Ctrl+P to print or save this page as PDF.

01Definition

From the current string choose one of the n one-flip neighbours uniformly at random (using the seeded generator) and move to it. Keep a record of the best string met on the way.

02Pseudocode

N ← start;  bestSeen ← N
repeat k times
    i ← random bit index
    N ← N with bit i flipped            # always accepted
    if h(N) > h(bestSeen): bestSeen ← N
return bestSeen

03Line by line

i ← random bit indexUniform over the n variables; the same seed always gives the same walk.
N ← flip(N, i)Accepted unconditionally, uphill, sideways or downhill.
track bestSeenThe walk itself may leave a good string at once, so the best one seen is what we return.

04Formulas and why they hold

P(pick bit i) = 1/n

every neighbour equally likely

That is what 'uniformly at random' means; h plays no part in the choice.

05Why it works, and when it does not

It is the baseline against which the other stochastic methods are judged: stochastic hill climbing and simulated annealing keep the randomness but let h bias the acceptance. On the ten-variable example sat-ten-var, 30 steps from 0000000000 find a solution for only 7 of 100 seeds, against 51 of 100 for simulated annealing (seed 2: the walk ends at h 46 of 48, annealing solves it).

06Properties

completeno
optimalno
timek steps
spaceO(n)

07Traps

  • Different seeds give different walks; compare methods over several seeds.
  • It may step straight off a solution; return bestSeen, not the last string.
  • It can revisit the same strings: the walk has no memory (tabu search adds one).
