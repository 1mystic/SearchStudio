Beam search

Run it step by step

Hill climbing follows a single path and so can be trapped. Beam search keeps a small population of w assignments: the w best of everything reachable in one flip from the current beam. A wider beam explores more routes at the cost of w times more work per level.

⎙Tip: press Ctrl+P to print or save this page as PDF.

01Definition

Level 0 is [start]. At each level, generate the N1 neighbours of every assignment in the beam (removing duplicates), sort them by h, and keep the best w. Stop when no solution appears and the best h of the new beam is not higher than that of the old one; return the best assignment seen.

02Pseudocode

OPEN ← [start]                       # the beam, best first
loop
    if some X in OPEN has h(X) = m: return X
    cand ← distinct N1 neighbours of every X in OPEN
    OPEN ← the w best of cand by h (ties: order of generation, lowest bit first)
    if h(head of OPEN) ≤ h(previous head): stop
return the best assignment seen

03Line by line

OPEN ← [start]The beam; its first element is the head.
if some X in OPEN is a solution: return XGoal test when the assignment is taken from the beam.
cand ← distinct neighbours of the beamTwo beam members can share a neighbour; keep it once.
OPEN ← the w best of candTies keep generation order: beam order first, then lowest bit.
stop if the head did not improveOtherwise the next level starts from the new beam.

04Formulas and why they hold

|cand| ≤ w · n

at most w·n candidates per level

The work per level is linear in the beam width.

w = 1 ⇒ hill climbing; w ≥ 2ⁿ ⇒ breadth-first over the whole space

the width interpolates between the two

The same dial as in graph and TSP beam search.

05Why it works, and when it does not

The beam lets a runner-up survive. Hill climbing keeps only the single best neighbour, but the second-best string may have a neighbour that beats everything reachable from the best one. On the example 'Local-maximum trap' (sat-local-trap) from 0000: w = 1 is hill climbing and stops at 0100 (h 7); w = 2 also keeps 0001 (h 7), and its neighbour 0011 has h 8, a solution, at level 2. The beam is still incomplete: it stops as soon as the head does not improve, so it cannot cross a plateau, and if all w members fall into the same basin it stalls like hill climbing. On the course example sat-four-var even w = 4 stops at 1000 (h 5), because the head of the beam does not improve at level 2.

06Properties

completeno
optimalno

07Traps

  • The beam holds the w best of all neighbours together, not w neighbours per member.
  • Remove duplicate neighbours, or the beam fills up with copies of the same string.
  • Stopping rule: the head not improving. A level whose beam is merely as good as the last one ends the search, so a wider beam does not help on a plateau (try sat-four-var with w = 1 to 4).
  • A wider beam is not always better; it costs w times more evaluations and only helps when a runner-up leads somewhere the best member does not.
