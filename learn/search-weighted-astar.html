Weighted A*

Run it step by step

Weighted A* multiplies the heuristic by a factor w ≥ 1. With w = 1 it is A*; with a large w it behaves like Best First Search. You trade optimality for speed, and the trade is controlled: the path is never worse than w times the best one.

⎙Tip: press Ctrl+P to print or save this page as PDF.

01Definition

OPEN is ordered on f(N) = g(N) + w·h(N). Everything else is as in A*: a goal test when a node is picked, and cheaper paths update the pair (node, parent, g). If h is admissible (h ≤ h*), the cost of the path returned satisfies cost ≤ w · C*, where C* is the optimal cost. The tracer computes C* with a separate Uniform Cost Search and checks the bound at the end.

02Pseudocode

OPEN ← [(S, nil, g=0)] ordered by f = g + w·h
while OPEN not empty
    N ← node with least f;  remove it
    if GoalTest(N): return the path               # cost ≤ w · C*  (admissible h)
    move N to CLOSED
    for each child C with g' = g(N) + k(N,C)
        new: add to OPEN
        cheaper than its pair on OPEN/CLOSED: update parent and g (reopen if CLOSED)

04Formulas and why they hold

f(N) = g(N) + w·h(N)

cost so far plus w times the estimate

w > 1 inflates h, which pulls the search toward the goal.

cost(path) ≤ w · C* (h admissible, w ≥ 1)

at most w times the optimum

w·h(N) ≤ w·h*(N), so w·h is within a factor w of an admissible estimate, and the first goal picked can be only that much worse than the best.

05Why it works, and when it does not

Fewer expansions than A* on many graphs, at the price of a controlled loss of optimality. It is a good compromise when A* is too slow. The bound is a guarantee, not a prediction: the actual path is often optimal.

06Properties

completeyes
optimalno: within w · C* with admissible h

07Traps

  • The bound needs an admissible h. With an inadmissible h there is no guarantee at all (try the inadmissible example).
  • w = 1 gives plain A*; w < 1 is not weighted A* (it drifts toward UCS and loses the bound meaning).
  • A larger w does not always expand fewer nodes on every graph; it usually does.
  • The trade in numbers: on the 'Dijkstra demo' graph w = 2 returns S–C–G at cost 9 (optimum 8, bound 2 × 8 = 16) after 3 expansions instead of A*'s 5; on the 4×5 grid w = 2 expands 8 nodes instead of 20 and still finds cost 7. Set w = 1 to get A* back.
