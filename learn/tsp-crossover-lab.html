Week 4 · TSP · genetic

Crossover lab

Run it step by step

Genetic algorithms combine two good tours into a child. For the TSP that is tricky: cutting two city lists and gluing the halves usually repeats some cities and loses others. TSP crossovers are designed to always produce valid tours.

01Definition

A crossover takes parent tours P1, P2 and builds a child that inherits structure from both: positions (cycle crossover), a segment plus positions (PMX), a segment plus relative order (order crossover), edges (alternating edges, heuristic crossover), or a prefix in ordinal code (ordinal crossover).

02Pseudocode

CX:  follow P1[i] → city P2[i] → its position in P1 …; odd cycles from P1, even from P2
PMX: copy P1[a..b]; elsewhere take P2's city, following the a..b mapping while it is taken
OX:  copy P1[a..b]; fill the blanks left to right with P2's order minus those cities
AEX: next city from P1, then P2, then P1 …; on a cycle use the other parent, else first unvisited
HX:  from each city take the shorter of the two parents' next edges
Ordinal: encode each tour by indices into the shrinking city list; single-point crossover; decode

03Line by line

CX: trace cyclesFrom position i, look at P2[i], find that city's position in P1, repeat until back at i. Odd cycles from P1, even from P2.
PMX: copy a segment, then mapFill other positions from P2; if the city is already in the segment, follow the segment mapping until a free city appears.
OX: copy a segment, fill in P2's orderThe remaining cities appear in the order they have in P2.
AEX: alternate parents' edgesNext city from P1, then P2, …; repair cycles.
HX: shorter parent edgeFrom each city take whichever parent's next city is closer.
Ordinal: encode, cut, decodeIndex into the shrinking list of unvisited cities; any cut gives a valid child.

04Formulas and why they hold

ordinal gene i ∈ {1, …, n−i+1}

the i-th index points into the n−i+1 cities still unvisited

Any mix of two such sequences is still a legal sequence, so single-point crossover is always valid.

adjacency: a[X] = city after X

store who comes next instead of the order

Tour cost depends on edges, so operators that pass on edges pass on the useful information.

05Why it works, and when it does not

Each operator preserves a different property of the parents. Since cost depends on edges, edge- and order-preserving operators usually produce better children than position-preserving ones.
