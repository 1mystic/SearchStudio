Branch & bound (exact)

Run it step by step

Brute force looks at every tour. Branch and bound looks at families of tours: a node stands for 'every tour that contains these edges and avoids those edges', and a cheap lower bound says how good any tour in the family could possibly be. If even the optimistic bound cannot beat the best tour already found, the whole family is discarded unseen.

⎙Tip: press Ctrl+P to print or save this page as PDF.

01Definition

A node is a pair (included edges, excluded edges). Its lower bound is ½ Σ over cities of the cost of the two cheapest admissible edges at that city: every tour uses exactly two edges at each city, and each edge is counted from both ends, hence the half. Branching picks an undecided edge e and creates two children: include e and exclude e. A node is a complete tour when every city has two included edges.

02Pseudocode

best ← ∞ (or the cost of a known tour);  OPEN ← [root: no edge decided]
while OPEN is not empty
    N ← pick a node from OPEN                       # DFS: newest;  best-first: lowest bound
    LB(N) ← ½ · Σ over cities of the two cheapest admissible edges
             (included edges first; excluded edges, edges at a city that already has 2 included
              edges, and edges closing a short cycle are not admissible)
    if LB(N) ≥ best: prune N
    else if every city has 2 included edges: best ← cost (a complete tour)
    else  e ← cheapest undecided edge in the bound
          children: N + include e,  N + exclude e   (drop infeasible ones)
return best

03Line by line

N ← pick from OPENDepth-first takes the newest node (include child first); best-first takes the node with the lowest bound.
LB(N) ← ½ Σ two cheapest admissible edgesIncluded edges at a city come first, then the cheapest admissible others. Excluded edges, edges at a city that already has two included edges, and edges that would close a sub-cycle are skipped.
if LB(N) ≥ best: pruneEvery tour under N costs at least LB(N); if that is not below the best tour found, N cannot improve on it.
every city has 2 included edgesThe included edges form one Hamiltonian cycle: a complete tour whose cost equals the bound.
children: include e / exclude eTogether they cover all the tours of N; a child that forces a city to degree 3, closes a short cycle, or leaves a city with fewer than two admissible edges is infeasible.

04Formulas and why they hold

LB = ½ Σ_c (e₁(c) + e₂(c))

half the sum over cities of the two cheapest admissible edges at c

Any tour has two edges at each city and each edge touches two cities, so its cost is half the sum of those per-city costs; choosing the cheapest two at every city can only lower that sum.

prune if LB ≥ best

the optimistic cost is not below the incumbent

No tour in this family can be strictly better than the best tour already found.

05Why it works, and when it does not

The bound costs O(n²) per node but can remove huge subtrees. It is tight when the cheap edges at each city fit together into one tour, and weak when they do not, which is why the order in which edges are decided and a good first tour matter.

06Properties

completeyes

07Traps

  • The bound is half the sum, not the sum.
  • An included edge counts for both of its cities; a city with two included edges contributes exactly those two.
  • An edge at a city that already has two included edges is not admissible for its other end either.
  • A bound can be a fraction (x.5); compare it with integer tour costs without rounding.
  • Pruning depends on the best tour found so far. On Six cities depth-first picks 68 nodes, finds five successively better tours (238, 237, 233, 229, 224) and prunes 24 subtrees; best-first picks 49 nodes and finds only the optimum; depth-first with Initial best 229 picks 55. The Initial best must be above the optimum: below it no tour is found.
