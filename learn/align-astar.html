Week 6 · Sequence alignment

Sequence alignment (A*)

Run it step by step

Dynamic programming fills the whole table. A* treats alignment as a shortest-path search on the same lattice and, with a good heuristic, explores only the part of the table near the best alignment.

01Definition

States are lattice points (i,j); moves are diagonal, down and right with their costs. h(i,j) = gap × |(m−i) − (n−j)| because the remaining length difference must be covered by gaps.

02Pseudocode

start (0,0), goal (m,n)
moves: diagonal cost(aᵢ,bⱼ), down gap, right gap
h(i,j) = gap · |(m − i) − (n − j)|           # admissible: the length difference must be covered by gaps
run A*: pick least f = g + h from OPEN; update cheaper paths; stop when (m,n) is picked

03Line by line

start (0,0), goal (m,n)
h = gap · |remaining length difference|Admissible and consistent.
A* with OPEN and CLOSEDStop when (m,n) is picked from OPEN.

04Formulas and why they hold

h(i,j) = gap · |(m − i) − (n − j)|

at least this many gaps are unavoidable

Each gap shortens the length difference by one, so this many gap moves remain in any completion: h never overestimates.

05Why it works, and when it does not

Optimal like dynamic programming, usually with fewer states. With large sequences, frontier search and DCFS keep its memory small.

06Properties

completeyes
optimalyes
time≤ O(m·n log)
space≤ O(m·n)

07Traps

  • A weak h (such as 0) makes A* behave like Dijkstra and expand most of the table.
